Background · slide 2

How often do you have to look to catch a paroxysmal episode?

Intermittent events such as paroxysmal atrial fibrillation or nocturnal hypoglycemia come and go. The review's premise is that episodic clinic visits miss them; this model puts numbers on that claim.

Probability of recording at least one episode

One simulated year: episodes (ticks) and what each strategy records

Model. Episodes arrive at random (Poisson, rate λ) and last a fixed time d. A recording window of length T captures an episode if they overlap, so P(capture) = 1 − e−λ(T+d). A 10-second clinic ECG is almost a point sample, so each visit catches an episode only with probability ≈ the fraction of time spent in one. Wear time is treated as one continuous block per year. Parameters are yours to set; they are not from the review.

Methods · power · slide 9

Power budget → battery life

The review reports the power a wearable spends per analysis at each stage of the field's evolution. Dividing a battery's energy by that draw shows why edge AI and neuromorphic hardware matter.

Highlight a power tier

Runtime on one charge (log scale)

Assumptions. Constant average draw = tier power × duty share; energy = mAh × 3.7 V. Ignores sensors, radio bursts, voltage conversion losses and self-discharge, so real runtimes are shorter. Tier values from the review: 100 mW per analysis cycle for earlier cloud-dependent systems and <5 mW with TinyML / spiking networks (§2), <1 mW for continuous multi-analyte monitoring (§2), <10 µW neuromorphic inference targeted for 2027–29 (§7).
